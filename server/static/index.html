POLYGRAPH

MINISTRY OF STATEMENTS

Hooly published 20,000 statements this year. Some of them lie.

1/4 · THE PROBLEM

20,000 claims. Some of them lie.

Checking them by hand takes weeks. An LLM would guess, and this world contradicts the web: only what Hooly's own pages say counts.

Polygraph is an automatic inspector for anyone who must check what companies claim: auditors, analysts, newsrooms. It checks each claim against the timeline of everything else said about it. No LLM, no internet, every verdict explained.

STEP 1 · INPUTthe row and its page
THE DECISION

Keep the page and the date

Each statement keeps its page and its date: lies cluster on pages, and truth changes over time. Keep an eye on that date.

STEP 2 · WHOlinker · Hooly's lexicon
THE DECISION

An exact lookup, no guessing who

Every name and alias in Hooly's lexicon, 62 of them, the longest match first. Hooly Pixel 10, underlined.

STEP 3 · WHICH VALUEreader · the organisers' rules
THE DECISION

Rules, not a model

A clone of the organisers' own value rules: money, dates, counts, versions, durations. Every example in those rules is one of our tests. €799 → money · 799 · EUR.

STEP 4 · WHICH FACTattributor · a trained model
THE DECISION

A model, where rules can't decide

Gradient-boosted trees, trained on 2,400 practice facts, score each of the 52 drawers the value could belong to. Pixel 10 · price wins with 99.3%.

STEP 5 · ON FILE?knowledge base
THE DECISION

Let what's known fix slips

A value that exactly matches what is on file for this date corrects an extraction slip. Lies never match, so it only ever rescues the truth. Nothing to correct here.

STEP 6 · WHENtruth engine · your turn
THE DECISION

Truth has a timeline

Every document about this fact goes on one timeline: a staircase fitted by dynamic programming, anchored by the sealed rows. Drag the pin and watch the same sentence change verdict.

THE TWIST

Same sentence, different truth

The archive holds the very same words, sealed true on 28 April. That's why we judge facts on their date, never the wording.

STEP 7 · VERDICTjudge · 64 clues
THE DECISION

Learned only from the labelled rows

64 clues, from the timeline, the page and the wording, go into decision trees trained only on labelled rows. Its confidence is calibrated on its own mistakes. P(false) = 98.2%.

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

  • It judges facts on their date, not the wording.
  • No LLM: only what this world says counts.
  • Every verdict shows its evidence.
  • Calibrated confidence: 90% means about 90%.

4/4 · UNDER THE HOOD

How it's built, and what it still misses.

THE IMPLEMENTATION

  • Python, numpy, scikit-learn: one Docker image.
  • 19,000 statements in 200 s (limit 1,800 s).
  • Memory 0.8 GB (limit 6 GB). No network.
  • Labels right on held-out rows: 88% (all-true: 83%).
  • New claims: 1,000 · 992 true when checked.
  • Docker vs. trace: 400 of 400 labels identical.

WHAT IT STILL MISSES

  • Catches 45% of the lies in testing (126 of 277).
  • It slips on ambiguity: which quarter's revenue?
  • Rumours are rarely called unsure (2 of 39).

NEXT
